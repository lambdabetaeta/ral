# `gather { … }`: one ownership mechanism, and `par` moves to the prelude

> Amended 2026-10-21:
> - **Unique `ScopeOwner` and closed membership.** A gather (`Frame::Gather` or
>   a worker's base) owns a unique `ScopeOwner` over its `Brood`. When the
>   gather begins exiting (return, halt, `Frame::abandon`, or `Drop` during
>   panic/stack-overflow unwind), it closes membership first (`ScopeOwner::close`
>   any late birth from a pipeline stage thread still unwinding against that
>   cradle is cancelled and rejected by `Brood::enrol` (`the enclosing gather
>   has ended; the new worker was cancelled`), and `ScopeOwner`'s `Drop`
>   cancels all remaining children if the popped frame's join panics during
>   `exit_brood`.
> - **Total exit drain across all accepted children.** `ScopeOwner::drain`
>   waits, within one shared cleanup grace, for every child accepted into the
>   gather to reach a terminal state (`ready` or `consumed`), including
>   children cancelled earlier by `cancel $h` inside the body (`stopping`), and
>   `race` likewise drains any argument already in `stopping`.
> - **`worker <id>` is deleted, and `first-settled` / `poll` observe the 4-state
>   handle lifecycle.** `first-settled` skips `stopping` and `consumed` handles
>   and fails only when none is `running` or `ready`; `poll` is a 4-arm total
>   observation over `running`, `stopping`, `ready` and `consumed`. Prelude
>   `par` keeps its sliding-window scheduler over `gather`, `spawn`,
>   `first-settled`, `poll` (`ready` arm) and `await`.

**A frame owns the workers born under it, and there is one mechanism for
that: the brood. `gather { … }` gives it syntax.** A `gather` installs a brood
on the current run exactly as a worker's base does: a `spawn` or `watch` inside
it is the gather's, joined at its return and cancelled and drained on a halt.
A worker's body *is* an implicit gather. The second ownership machine — a
`Litter` over call-owned births, behind the Rust `par` and the `all` form — is
deleted. `par` is prelude code over `gather`, `spawn`, a new pure observation
`first-settled`, `poll` and `await`, and keeps every property it had; `all` is
removed, `both` covering the two-ary case; `first-of` races fresh blocks;
`race` stays in Rust for handles already held.

This amends
[[decisions/261019_await-is-the-block-run-here|await-is-the-block-run-here]]:
it amends I2, replaces I3–I5 and adds I4′. I1 and I6–I10 stand in letter; the
owner they name changes.

## Decision

- **I2, two data observations.** `poll` is the data-valued observation of one
  handle, total on an unclaimed one, and `first-settled` the data-valued
  observation of several: the index of the first to settle. Neither writes,
  neither claims. Both treat a claimed handle as `await` does: `poll` of one
  fails, `first-settled` skips it and fails only when every handle it was
  given is claimed.
- **I3, three owners, one of them a scope.** Every concurrent block has
  exactly one owner, fixed at birth:
  - a *gather* — a `gather { … }` form, or a worker's body. Its births hang
    under the scope current *when the gather was entered* (a `within` inside
    the body does not re-parent them), so a foreground interrupt or wall on
    the run reaches them, and it ends them at its own exit;
  - the *session* — `spawn`/`watch` with no brood in scope (a bare run, an
    aside): hangs at the session root, ended by completion, `cancel`, or the
    session's end;
  - *nobody* — `detach`.

  There is no fourth owner and no lease. A `watch` inside a gather is the
  gather's, exactly as a `spawn` is.
- **I4, a gather ends with its children.** The body ends, then each child no
  one has claimed is claimed and awaited in birth order with I1's semantics, a
  child another claimer won being skipped; the first failure is the gather's,
  and the rest are cancelled and drained within the cleanup grace. A body that
  halts cancels and drains them; one that panics cancels them without waiting.
  A worker's base is this rule applied to its own brood. *A normal return
  joins; it never cancels.*
- **I4′, the exit drain is total.** A gather's exit also waits, bounded by the
  grace, for every child it started whose scope is cancelled and whose thread
  has not ended, however it was cancelled: the halt rule, a `cancel` in the
  body, or an outside `cancel` through `worker <id>`. A child an outside party
  claimed and is legitimately awaiting, uncancelled, is left to that party.
- **I5, hereditary cancellation.** A gather-owned birth's node is a child of
  the gather's entry scope; a session birth's node a child of the session
  root. `cancel $h` reaches everything `$h` started; an interrupt or a wall on
  a run reaches that run's gathers' children, never a session worker.

I6 is untouched: a gather is not a worker base and converts nothing; a
gather's *child* is a worker and converts as any worker does.

The surface, after:

```text
gather BODY        : control operator.  BODY : U(F^ε α);  the form : F^ε α
spawn [TITLE] BODY : owned by the enclosing gather if one is in scope, else the session
first-settled : ∀ε α. [Handle^ε α] → F Int     index of the first to settle; claims nothing
race    : ∀ε α. [Handle^ε α] → F^ε α            kept; drains losers it does not own
workers : F [[…, owner: <session | worker: Int | gather>, …]]
par     : ∀α β. U(α → F β) → [α] → Int → F [β]  prelude
both    : U(F α) → U(F β) → F [fst: α, snd: β]  prelude
first-of: [U(F^ε α)] → F^ε α                    prelude
```

## Why one mechanism

- **The two machines were one idea.** A call-owned birth already hung under
  the caller's current scope, used the run's live structured surface, was
  registered and announced nothing, and buffered its bytes per handle — what
  a gather-owned birth needs. `Birth::Call` is subsumed: a birth has an owner
  when a brood is in scope, and the owner, not the verb, decides how it is
  announced and delivered.
- **The owner announces and delivers, uniformly.** A session birth is the
  session's errand: announced, its structured surface buffered to the
  deferred sink, one `` `done `` at completion. A gather-owned birth — a
  worker's child included — is registered (the cap bounds it, `workers` lists
  it), uses the run's live surface, and emits no `` `done ``: its result
  reaches the session through its owner. One unit, one notice.
- **`par` needs no output-order compromise.** Scheduling and claiming are
  different acts. `first-settled` says *which* child settled and `poll`
  *how*, neither claiming; a settled child's bytes sit in its handle until
  claimed. So prelude `par` starts the next item the moment any slot frees,
  raises the first failure the moment it sees it, and still awaits every
  child in input order at the end: bytes and values in input order.
- **`race` stays Rust.** Over handles started earlier it must drain losers
  that no gather owns; `cancel` is fire-and-forget, so only `race`'s own
  `end_unclaimed` bounds that drain. `first-of` is the race over fresh blocks.
- **`all` goes.** Not for typing — it was a form, typed field by field — but
  for surface economy: two syntaxes for one mechanism is what this removes.
  `both` and the `gather` pattern cover its uses.
- **A handle that escapes its gather is left as it is.** The gather claims
  the child at exit, so a later `await` fails as already claimed: truthful and
  actionable, without the handle carrying its gather's identity.

## Reversed

- **"A `gather { … }` delimiter" was rejected** by
  [[decisions/261019_await-is-the-block-run-here|await-is-the-block-run-here]]
  as a fourth owner, redundant with `par`/`all`/`race`. Both halves fall: a
  gather is not a fourth owner but the worker's own scope given syntax, the
  same brood under a second entrance, so it *replaces* the call owner rather
  than joining it; and the redundancy argument assumed `par`/`all`/`race` as
  primitives, which they no longer are once `par` and its siblings are
  prelude code over `gather`. One ownership mechanism beats two.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Deleting `race`.** Racing held handles would silently stop draining its
  losers.
- **A sharper refusal for an escaped handle**, which needs the handle to carry
  its gather's identity for one diagnostic.
- **`all` as a desugaring to the `gather` pattern.** Possible, and a small
  separate change if the record form is missed; not taken now.
- **`Option<WorkerId>` or `Owner` as a brood's owner.** The first encodes
  "gather" as an absence, the second admits `Session` in a brood;
  `BroodOwner` has exactly the two cases.

## Consequences

- A `spawn` inside a worker announces no birth and flushes no `` `done `` of
  its own; a long worker's internal child is visible only through `workers`
  until the worker joins it.
- A brood prunes a child only once it is claimed *and* ended, so a
  cancelled-but-running child stays visible to I4′.
- I4′ is cooperative: a child that ignores cancellation outlives its gather
  by the grace, as a `guard` cleanup and the session wipe already accept.
- `par`'s failures name the builtin that met them (`await:`, `spawn:`), not
  `par`: the cost of being library code.
- `gather` joins the reserved words; `all` leaves.
- Amends [[decisions/261019_await-is-the-block-run-here|await-is-the-block-run-here]]
  and [[decisions/261016_delimiters-are-handlers-for-poll|delimiters-are-handlers-for-poll]]
  (its construct table); law 9 and `convert_own_stop` are unchanged.
